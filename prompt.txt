You are designing the UI for a hackathon product called ShoreCheck.

WHAT IT IS
ShoreCheck shows the official water quality status of Halifax's supervised
beaches on a map, along with local events happening near those beaches, plus
a chat assistant named Hugo who recommends a beach based on where you are,
when you are going, and whether you want somewhere peaceful or somewhere
busy. It is a Next.js site with Tailwind, deployed on Vercel.

THREE SCREENS

1. Landing page. Navbar: ShoreCheck wordmark on the left, two links on the
right, "Dashboard" and "Talk to Hugo". Hero is a left/right split. Left side
is the product name and a one line pitch: "Want to know the perfect beach to
go to based on your preferences? We got you." Right side is an iOS style
phone frame containing a static screenshot of the dashboard. The phone is
decorative, not interactive.

2. Dashboard. A map of the Halifax region taking most of the viewport. Beach
markers colored by water quality status, four states: open, advisory,
closed, closed for season. Event markers in a visually distinct shape.
Clicking a beach marker opens a small card with its status, the raw test
result, and a plain language line. Clicking an event marker opens a short
blurb with a control that expands into a full event panel. Below the map, a
scrollable row or grid of upcoming event cards.

3. Hugo. A single column chat interface. Hugo's opening message asks whether
the user wants somewhere peaceful or somewhere happening, offered as two
tappable chips, then it continues as ordinary text chat.

BEFORE YOU DESIGN ANYTHING, INTERROGATE ME
Ask your questions in batches of no more than three at a time, as
multiple-choice option boxes rather than open prose questions, with a short
"other, let me type it" escape on each. Keep going until you can design
confidently. Then summarise what you have concluded and wait for me to
confirm before you produce a single screen.

Cover at least: overall mood and any reference products; how literal the
coastal theme should be; light mode, dark mode, or both; palette beyond the
four fixed status colours; typography; map tile style and how much UI chrome
floats over the map; density versus whitespace; how Hugo is personified
(avatar, name treatment, tone of his messages); mobile behaviour; and what
"finished" looks like given the whole thing has to be built in about three
hours.

CONSTRAINTS YOU MUST RESPECT
- Tailwind utility classes only. No custom design tokens I would have to
  wire up by hand.
- The map is react-leaflet with OpenStreetMap tiles, so the base map style
  is largely fixed. Design around it, not for a custom styled basemap.
- No illustrations that would need a designer to draw.
- Status must never be carried by colour alone. Pair every status colour
  with a shape, icon or text label so it stays readable for colourblind
  users. This is a public health signal.
- Everything must degrade to a single column on mobile without a separate
  layout.
